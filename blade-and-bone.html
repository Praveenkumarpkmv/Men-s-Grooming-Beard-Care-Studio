The Bone

Your Face Decides the Cut

Pick your face shape. We'll show you what works, what to avoid, and what to ask for.

Select Shape

Oval Face

The fortunate one. Slightly longer than wide, with a soft jaw and gentle curve. Almost any cut works.

Works Well

  • Classic side part or pompadour
  • Textured crop with mid fade
  • Buzz cut with sharp line-up
  • Full beard or clean shave

Avoid

  • Very short, flat crops that hide the natural balance
  • Heavy fringe that shortens the face

Round Face

Equal width and length, soft jawline, full cheeks. The trick is to add verticality and structure.

Avoid

Strong jaw, wide forehead, angular features. The job is to soften, not to sharpen further.

  • Soft side part, natural edges
  • Short rounded beard

Avoid

  • Hard, square hairline or hard part

The Sharpening Ritual

Four Steps, Every Week

A dull blade tears hair. A sharp blade cuts it clean. This is what we do between clients — every week, on every tool.

01

Clean

Every tool is stripped, wiped, and disinfected after each client.

02

Oil

Clipers and trimmers are oiled weekly. Blades get two drops, then run hot for 30 seconds.

03

Hone

Straight razors get the strop. Shears get the stone. Both get tested on paper before use.

04

Replace

Anything that doesn't pass the test gets retired. No blade goes into a cut that isn't 100%.
