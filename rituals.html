The Field Guide · Vol. 01

The modern grooming rituals.

The things you should be doing, the things you can stop doing, and exactly how to maintain what we built in the chair.

01min

The Cold Rinse

Water temperature does more than clean. Cool water closes the cuticle — the smallest habit with the biggest long-term payoff.

04drops

The Oil Rule

Four drops in the morning, five at night. Worked into the skin, not the hair. Most men use too much or none at all.

06weeks

The Break-In

A beard or a new cut takes six weeks to settle. Any advice worth following starts with patience.

Three Moments

Time of Day, Times Three

Click a card to see the steps. Each one takes less time than a coffee.

7:00 AM · 4 min

The Morning

The setup. Everything you do now decides how the day wears — how the hair sits, how the beard feels, how you feel walking out the door.

Tap to see steps
Morning Steps
  1. Cold water rinse — 60 seconds
  2. Wash (only if needed) — sulfate-free
  3. Towel pat — never rub
  4. Wide-tooth comb, growth direction
  5. 4 drops of beard oil, damp skin
— start slow, keep it short —
1:00 PM · 2 min

The Midday

The reset. A quick brush, cold hands, and a touch of balm. This is the ritual that keeps the cut looking sharp past lunch.

Tap to see steps
Midday Steps
  1. Cold hands first — helps the style hold
  2. Boar-bristle brush, 30 seconds
  3. Small amount of balm (if you beard)
  4. Quick once-over in a mirror
— a two-minute reset —
10:00 PM · 5 min

The Evening

The reset that matters most. Everything you do before bed decides what your hair and beard feel like when you wake up.

Tap to see steps
Evening Steps
  1. Rinse only — no shampoo at night
  2. Scalp massage — 2 minutes, fingertips
  3. 5 drops of oil, worked deep
  4. Wide-tooth comb straight down
  5. Satin pillowcase — worth it
— your best work happens asleep —

Field Notes

Things We've Learned

Short observations from behind the chair — the kind of thing that only comes up once you've cut hair for a decade.

"Most men over-wash their hair. If it feels dry, washing less is usually the answer — not a different product."

Marcus Hale
Founder · 12 years

"A beard oil is not for the beard. It's for the skin under it. Get that wrong and the beard pays for it."

Elias Moreno
Beard Specialist · 8 years

"Trim dry, never wet. Wet hair lies shorter than it looks. Everyone cuts too much on their first try."

Theo Bennett
Fade Specialist · 6 years
